About Dr Ahmad Mohsin

Dr Ahmad Mohsin is an academic researcher working at the intersection of Systems Engineering, Trustworthy Artificial Intelligence, Cybersecurity, Digital Twins, and resilient cyber-physical systems. He has over 15 years of experience across academia and industry, focusing on the design, modelling, simulation, verification, and security assurance of complex software-intensive systems.

Dr Mohsin is a Senior Researcher at the Centre for Securing Digital Futures (CSDF), Edith Cowan University, Australia, and contributes to national cybersecurity research initiatives through collaborations with research organisations and industry partners. He holds a PhD in Computer Science and Software Engineering from Edith Cowan University, Australia.

Systems Engineering and Systems-of-Systems Research

Dr Mohsin's research foundation is in Systems-of-Systems (SoS) engineering, software architecture modelling, stochastic simulation, and verification of complex adaptive systems. His doctoral research investigated approaches for modelling and analysing dynamic Systems-of-Systems architectures to understand system behaviour, reliability, performance, and evolution under changing operational conditions.

His systems research focuses on how interconnected autonomous components, software-intensive platforms, and physical assets can be engineered, simulated, and assured to achieve dependable operation. This foundation has evolved into research on cyber-physical systems, autonomous systems, Digital Twins, and resilient infrastructure.

Cybersecurity and Critical Infrastructure Resilience

Dr Mohsin has contributed to cybersecurity research addressing the protection of critical infrastructure and operational technology environments. Through the Augmenting Cyber Defense Capabilities (ACDC) project, he contributed to maritime cyber-physical system security, including system architecture modelling, Purdue Reference Architecture alignment, cyber incident response modelling, cyber wargaming scenarios, and threat analysis using the MITRE ATT&CK framework.

His cybersecurity research explores intelligent threat detection, knowledge-driven security analytics, secure-by-design systems, and resilience mechanisms for complex digital ecosystems. His work combines systems engineering approaches with cybersecurity practices to develop trustworthy and adaptive technologies.

Digital Twins, Industry 5.0 and Future Systems

Dr Mohsin investigates secure Digital Twin architectures that integrate simulation, semantic reasoning, artificial intelligence, and cybersecurity analytics to improve understanding and resilience of complex systems. His research explores how Digital Twins can support continuous monitoring, threat intelligence, assurance, and secure decision-making in industrial and critical infrastructure environments.

His recent research includes Industry 5.0 cybersecurity for advanced manufacturing systems and Trusted Sovereign Monitor approaches for resilient energy systems. These projects investigate how AI-enabled technologies can strengthen operational resilience, security, and sovereign capability in future infrastructure.

Trustworthy AI and Human–AI Collaboration

Dr Mohsin's current research explores trustworthy artificial intelligence, Large Language Models (LLMs), generative AI security, AI safety, and human–AI collaboration. His work investigates how emerging AI technologies can be safely integrated into operational environments while maintaining reliability, appropriate autonomy, and human oversight.

His research includes evaluating the security and reliability of LLM-generated code, developing AI-grounded cybersecurity knowledge systems, and designing trusted autonomy approaches for Security Operations Centres. These contributions support responsible adoption of AI in security-critical environments.

Research Translation and Industry Engagement

Dr Mohsin actively collaborates with academic institutions, research organisations, government partners, and industry. He works with Australian industry through research translation activities, including mentoring organisations through the CSIRO Data61 Innovate to Grow program, supporting the development of research-driven proof-of-concepts and innovative technology solutions.

He welcomes collaboration opportunities with universities, R&D organisations, Defence and critical infrastructure stakeholders, and technology partners interested in trustworthy AI, cybersecurity, Digital Twins, Systems-of-Systems engineering, and resilient cyber-physical systems.
